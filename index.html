Stanford Robotics Lab · 2013–2014

Haptic surgical simulation for facial trauma

Haptic renderingForce feedbackCollision & contactSurgical simulationMedical training

A computer-aided trauma simulation system, built with surgeons, in which they manipulate virtual bone fragments through haptic devices, with real-time force feedback from contact and collisions. It was used to plan jaw fracture repair operations and to train surgeons.

User studies showed that haptic feedback improved surgeons' experience and the accuracy of fracture reduction, and that the system was easy and fast for oral-maxillofacial surgeons to learn and use.

Journal of Rehabilitation Research and Development, 2016 →
Journal of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, 2014 →

Facial physics: muscle and fat

Learned facial muscle regions by k-means clustering on eigenvectors (PCA) of the blendshapes, grouping vertices that move similarly, then added muscle and fat soft-tissue simulation using FEM, projective dynamics and position-based dynamics.

MPC · SIGGRAPH 2016 poster

Example-based data optimization for facial simulation

From artist-sculpted blendshapes, built a tetrahedral FEM model of the tissue between skin and skull, then estimated control forces and Neo-Hookean material parameters with regularised Levenberg-Marquardt (nonlinear least squares) so the simulation reproduces the animators' expressions. At runtime the face responds to collisions and inertia.

About

After 15 years building virtual worlds for film and games, I want to bring that work back to the real world. My background covers the computational problems underneath robotics: collision detection and proximity queries, rigid-body and deformable simulation, haptic rendering, and fast, memory-efficient real-time systems in C++.

I'm now focused on simulation for robotics and on training robot policies in simulation, and I'm based in London.
